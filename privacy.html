Privacy Policy

Effective November 24, 2026. In short: your information stays on your computer, except for what you post to the accounts you connect.

What Schedora keeps

Schedora has no servers and no user accounts of its own. It keeps the following on your computer:

  • Your schedule: captions, dates, the accounts and brands you choose, whether each post went out, and alerts.
  • Your media: copies of images and videos you add, or only their location if you choose to leave them in place.
  • Connected accounts: each account’s ID and its name or username, and for Pinterest the names of your boards. It also keeps when a sign-in was made, renewed or expires.
  • Sign-ins: the access tokens given by each service, kept in Windows Credential Manager. Schedora doesn’t store your social media passwords.
  • Developer app details and settings: app IDs, app secrets (in Windows Credential Manager) and preferences such as your theme.

How it’s used

Only to connect your accounts, show your schedule, and publish or send the posts you plan. There is no advertising, tracking, analytics or profiling, and nothing is used to train AI models.

What leaves your computer

  • To the services you connect: sign-in requests, and the captions and media of posts you schedule or publish there. For example, TikTok gets the video for your draft, and Pinterest gets your Pin’s image or video, text and board.
  • For Instagram images: each image is briefly added to one of your Facebook Pages as a hidden, unpublished photo so Instagram can fetch it. Schedora then deletes that photo.
  • To websites whose links you add: a request to download that file.

Nothing is sold, and nothing is sent to Schedora’s developer. Each service handles what it receives under its own policy, including those of TikTok, Pinterest, Google (which runs YouTube), Meta, LinkedIn, X and Bluesky.

YouTube and Google information

Schedora uses YouTube API Services to list your channels and upload your videos. Its use of information received from Google APIs follows the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

Keeping and deleting it

Information stays until you remove it:

  • Disconnect an account in Connections to delete its sign-in from your computer.
  • Delete posts and media in the app.
  • To remove everything, disconnect your accounts, remove Schedora’s entries from Windows Credential Manager, uninstall the app and delete its folders, %APPDATA%\com.schedora.desktop and %LOCALAPPDATA%\com.schedora.desktop.
  • To withdraw access on a service’s side, remove Schedora in that service’s settings. On TikTok that’s under app permissions, and on Google at Google’s permissions page.

Posts already sent to a service stay there until you delete them there.

Security

Sign-ins and secrets are kept in Windows Credential Manager, and connections to the services use HTTPS. Anyone who can use your Windows account may be able to open Schedora, so keep it locked.

Children

Schedora isn’t meant for children under 13, or under the minimum age for the services you connect.
